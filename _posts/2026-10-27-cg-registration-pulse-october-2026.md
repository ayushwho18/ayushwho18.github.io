**In one line:** Raipur's festive-season registrations halved this year, the new guideline rates are still working their way through the market, and the Centre is looking at plugging land records into the lending stack.

This is the first edition of a monthly note on what changed in Chhattisgarh's property registration, using public sources only. Views are my own.

## What changed

**Raipur's Navratri registrations halved (reported 22 October).** About 500 documents were registered at the Raipur registry over Navratri, against about 900 last year, and revenue fell from about ₹14 crore to about ₹7 crore, [according to Patrika](https://www.patrika.com/raipur-news/registry-revenue-decline-property-registration-new-guideline-21000365). Two reasons are given. The office was open only five of the nine days, because of a Sunday start, weekends and the Ashtami holiday. And buyers are still adjusting to the new guideline rates. The Registrar expects numbers to pick up around Diwali.

**The guideline-rate reset is still rolling out.** New guideline rates took effect statewide on 20 November 2025, the first broad revision in about six years, [reported ETV Bharat](https://www.etvbharat.com/hi/state/chhattisgarh-new-land-guideline-rates-2025-26-chhattisgarh-news-cts25121305227). Its report from Surguja describes average increases above 100% for rural land along main roads. Districts were then allowed to propose local corrections. The Central Valuation Board approved revised rates for Bilaspur, Korea and Sarangarh-Bilaigarh from 13 February 2026, with other districts to follow in phases, [per INH News](https://www.inhnews.in/news/new-land-guideline-rates-approved-in-chhattisgarh).

**Raipur is planning seven more registry offices (reported 21 September).** The administration is looking for buildings in outer areas such as Tatibandh-Hirapur, Amlidih-Avanti Vihar and Birgaon-Siltara. That would take registration to 14 locations in the capital, which handles about 400 registrations a day, [reported IBC24](https://www.ibc24.in/chhattisgarh/chhattisgarh-land-registry-latest-news-3764322.html). No opening date has been given.

**Nationally: land records and lending may meet.** In July the Department of Land Resources met the RBI Innovation Hub to explore linking digital land records with the Unified Lending Interface. The ideas include consent-based access to authenticated records, mortgage flag-marking to stop the same parcel being pledged twice, and wider use of the ULPIN (Bhu-Aadhaar), [according to The Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/business/centre-explores-linking-digital-land-records-with-uli-to-improve-rural-credit-access-mord/). The discussions are exploratory; no policy has been announced.

## One number

**₹7 crore**, Raipur's Navratri registry revenue this year, half of last year's ₹14 crore. A short window explains part of the drop. The rest is a reminder that guideline rates are not just a revenue lever: they set the floor on what a buyer pays in stamp duty, so a sharp revision can pause the market before it raises collections.

## What it means for property data and lending

Guideline values now move district by district and in phases, not once a year for the whole state. Any product that estimates stamp duty, flags under-valued deeds or benchmarks collateral needs a district-level rate history with effective dates, not a single state table. The three districts revised in February show how easily a stale rate slips into a valuation.

The ULI discussion matters more for Chhattisgarh than it looks. Most of the state's land records are in Hindi, while loan files are in English, so any automated mortgage flag will only be as good as its name matching across scripts. That is the gap I built [naam-match](https://github.com/ayushwho18/naam-match) to make visible.

## What I'm watching next month

- Whether Diwali registrations recover in Raipur, and by how much.
- The next batch of district guideline revisions from the Central Valuation Board.
- Any ULI pilot that uses state land records, and which states go first.
